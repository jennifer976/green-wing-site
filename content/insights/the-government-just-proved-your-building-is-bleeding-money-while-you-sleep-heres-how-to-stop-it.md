## Your building may be working harder than your business is

Most commercial buildings do not switch off when people leave.

They go into a kind of “silent operating mode”.

Equipment continues to run. Systems continue to maintain temperatures. Lighting, ventilation, refrigeration, servers and pumps keep consuming energy, often with little oversight.

Some of that use is necessary.

A lot of it may not be.

This overnight demand is often referred to as **baseload energy use**: the electricity a building consumes when activity is low or the site is unoccupied.

Baseload can come from:

- Heating, ventilation and air-conditioning systems
- Refrigeration and cold storage
- Hot-water systems and circulation pumps
- Servers, networking equipment and IT infrastructure
- External and internal lighting
- Extraction systems
- Display equipment and retail refrigeration
- Security, access control and monitoring systems
- Machinery left in standby mode
- Poorly configured timers, controls or building-management systems

The issue is not that every system should be switched off overnight.

The issue is that many businesses do not know which systems are running, why they are running or whether they are running efficiently.

## The hospitality trial is a useful warning for every commercial operator

The government-backed hospitality trial was designed to help smaller pubs, restaurants, cafés and hotels use smart-meter data to identify energy and carbon-saving opportunities.

According to the [Department for Energy Security and Net Zero](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/britains-hospitality-sector-to-save-3-million-under-new-scheme), the scheme was expected to support more than 600 small and medium-sized hospitality businesses, with potential energy bill savings of more than £3 million and carbon reductions of around 2,700 tonnes.

The results show what can happen when businesses combine:

- Smart-meter data
- Energy monitoring
- Practical advice
- Staff engagement
- Better shutdown procedures
- More effective control of heating, lighting and equipment

For some sites, overnight electricity use fell by up to 66%.

That does not mean every building will achieve the same reduction. Site size, operating hours, equipment and occupancy patterns all matter.

But it does demonstrate something important:

**Energy waste is often hiding in the hours when nobody is looking.**

And that applies far beyond hospitality.

## What overnight waste looks like in different buildings 
<br>


### Offices

An office may appear to be empty at 8pm, but its energy profile can tell a different story.

Heating and cooling may continue operating across the whole building. Air-handling systems may be programmed around assumed occupancy rather than actual working patterns. Lighting may remain active in communal areas, meeting rooms or external spaces.

IT equipment is another factor. Servers and networking equipment may need to remain online, but printers, screens, kitchen appliances and other equipment may not.

<br>

### Gyms and leisure facilities

Gyms often have extended opening hours and substantial ventilation, heating and hot-water demands.

Even after the final member leaves, air-handling systems, pumps, lighting, hot-water circulation and refrigeration may continue running at daytime settings.

Small changes to operating schedules can sometimes produce meaningful savings without affecting the customer experience.

<br>

### Retail premises


Retailers may have refrigeration, signage, display lighting, security systems and HVAC running throughout the night.

Some of these loads are essential. Others may be operating because a timer was never updated, a control setting was inherited from a previous tenant or the building-management system is not being used effectively.

<br>

### Warehouses and industrial units

Warehouses can have large, high-bay lighting systems, heating systems, loading equipment, compressors, pumps and charging infrastructure.

The building may be empty for long periods, yet significant energy use continues.

The opportunity is often found by comparing actual operating patterns with intended occupancy and production schedules.

<br>

### Commercial landlords and property portfolios

For landlords, overnight baseload is not only a cost issue. It can also affect tenant relationships, asset performance and future compliance planning.

Common areas, plant rooms, vacant units and shared systems can all contribute to unnecessary consumption.

Across a portfolio, a small recurring issue at each property can become a substantial annual cost.

## The quickest savings are not always the most expensive ones

Energy efficiency is often associated with major capital projects.

Those projects can be worthwhile, but they should not automatically be the starting point.

In many buildings, the first opportunities are operational:

- Adjusting heating and cooling schedules
- Correcting time clocks
- Separating zones
- Improving start-up and shutdown procedures
- Reducing unnecessary lighting hours
- Changing standby settings
- Reviewing hot-water temperatures and circulation
- Making controls easier for staff to use
- Identifying equipment that is running outside required hours

These changes may require little or no capital expenditure.

They can also provide a clearer baseline before investing in larger interventions such as controls upgrades, LED lighting, solar generation, battery storage, heat pumps or refrigeration improvements.

The priority should be simple:

**Find the waste. Rank the options. Act in the right order. Measure the result.**
